3d · Voter Guide

The voter guide is managed race by race. For each race, add the candidates, their party, endorsement status, and any notes. The search and filtering on the public side are automatic — residents just type and the right races appear.

💡

The voter guide is most valuable when updated before each election cycle. Add candidates as soon as primaries are decided. Residents will return to the site specifically to look up their ballot before Election Day.

3e · Legislative Tracker

Add and update legislative actions — bills, votes, or policy decisions — by filling in plain-English fields: a title, a type (Delivered / Blocked by GOP / Harmful), a category (like Education or Healthcare), a one-sentence description, and a link to your source.

To make entries searchable by community, fill in the Communities affected field with the names of boroughs or neighborhoods that are directly impacted — for example Fox Chapel, Mt. Lebanon, or Squirrel Hill. Use the same spelling your committee uses in its search settings.

When a resident visits the tracker on a local committee site — say, the Fox Chapel Democrats site — they'll automatically see only the entries tagged for Fox Chapel. They can clear the filter at any time to browse everything. No technical knowledge needed.

Adding a New Committee — About 10 Minutes

Setting up a new committee site requires no technical help. A county admin fills out a form in the dashboard and clicks Publish. The site is live immediately.

  1. In the dashboard, go to Municipalities → New Municipality
  2. Enter the committee name and a short web ID (used in the URL — e.g. foxchapel)
  3. Toggle Is Active on
  4. Upload their logo and set their accent color
  5. Write a short hero headline for their homepage
  6. Enter their district or zip codes so the voter guide pre-filters for their residents
  7. Click Publish — the site is live instantly
🚀

You can demo the site to a committee before they've committed to anything — create it, publish it, share the link. No domain setup, no IT required.

5 · What It Costs

Based on your current traffic (~2,100 views/week), both services that power the site fit comfortably within their free tiers — even during election-season spikes. The one nuance is Vercel's terms of service.

Service What it does Free tier verdict Paid option
Sanity
Content editor & database		 Stores and delivers all content (events, news, voter guide, etc.) ✓ Free tier works. Pages are cached, so Sanity only gets called when content changes — not on every visitor request. Current traffic fits easily. $15/mo Growth plan if you need more than 2 editor accounts or hit storage limits
Vercel
Hosts the website		 Serves every page load to visitors ⚠ Technically fits, but ToS applies. Traffic (~9K views/mo, ~5 GB bandwidth) is well within the free Hobby plan's limits. However, Vercel's terms restrict Hobby to personal use — an organization should be on Pro. $20/mo Pro plan — appropriate for a committee/org, includes 100 GB bandwidth

Bottom line

Sanity is genuinely free at your scale. Vercel could run free technically, but the correct plan for an organization is Pro at $20/mo. Even a 5× election-season spike (~45K views/mo) stays well under Pro's limits — no overage charges expected.

Local committee sites — no extra hosting cost

Each municipality site runs on the same Vercel deployment. Adding Fox Chapel, Mt. Lebanon, or any other committee costs nothing extra in hosting — it's just a new domain pointed at the same app.
