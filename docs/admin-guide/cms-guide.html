The One Rule That Matters: Edit → Publish

Every change is a private draft until you publish it.

Every time you change something, the dashboard automatically saves your work as a draft. A draft is private — it does not appear on the public website yet. Your change only goes live when you click the green Publish button at the bottom-right of the screen.

You can edit and re-publish any item as many times as you like — each round of edits just needs its own Publish click. If you close the tab without publishing, your edits aren't lost: they're waiting as a draft the next time you open that item. They're simply not on the live site until you publish them.

⚠️

If a change isn't showing up on the website, the most likely reason is that it was saved as a draft but never published. Open the item again and click Publish.

Site Settings — Homepage, Navigation, Contact

One place for site-wide items: the homepage hero, the top menu, contact info, and social links. Each is a tab across the top of the Site Settings screen.

Homepage Hero

The homepage's big headline, the sentence under it, and the banner image live in Site Settings → Homepage Hero. If the fields are blank, the site shows sensible built-in defaults — so to take control, just fill them in and Publish.

🏠

There's no single "Homepage" button. The hero comes from Site Settings; the rest of the homepage (alerts, upcoming events, latest news) fills in automatically from your Action Alerts, Events, and News. The three "What the Committee Does" cards are fixed text — changing those needs a developer.

Navigation

The top menu is edited in Site Settings → Navigation. Each item has a label, a link, and optional dropdown sub-links.

⚠️

All-or-nothing: if Navigation Items is left empty, the site shows its built-in default menu. As soon as you add any item, your list replaces the whole menu — so enter every top-level item and dropdown you want, not just the new one. Two items are always added automatically and shouldn't be entered: Local Committees and Member Login.

Contact & Social

Office address, phone, email, office hours, Facebook page, and Instagram handle live in the Contact Info and Social Media tabs. They feed the footer, the contact page, and the homepage Facebook feed. The Technical / Integrations tab holds the Google Analytics ID and Facebook Pixel — leave those alone unless you know you need them.

Creating an event takes about two minutes. Fill in the title, date, location, and a short description. The options at the bottom control where it appears.

Election Day reminders, volunteer drives, major announcements — turn on "Share with all" and every active committee site shows it automatically. One post, everywhere.

News & Updates

Works exactly like events — write the article, optionally attach a photo, choose where it appears.

One extra option: Feature on Homepage pins the article to the top of the homepage news section. The same municipality/sharing controls apply: blank = county, pick a committee to scope it, or share with all committee sites.

Action Alerts

A prominent banner at the top of the site for time-sensitive calls to action.

Fill in the message, pick an urgency level (which sets the color — red for urgent, blue for take-action, gold for announcements), and optionally add a button label and link. Alerts use the same sharing controls as events: county-wide, one committee, or every committee site. Remove an alert by unpublishing it.

Voter Guide

Managed under Voter Guides, race by race. The public search — by street address, ZIP, or borough — is automatic.

Each race holds the office title, its districts, and the candidates — with party, ballot status, endorsement, and notes. When a resident enters their address, the site looks up their congressional, PA Senate, and PA House districts and shows only the races on their ballot. There's nothing to configure per resident.

For bulk updates, use the Candidate Finder tool (in the left menu, just under Voter Guides) to search and jump to candidates across the whole guide.

💡

The voter guide is most valuable when updated before each election cycle. Add candidates as soon as primaries are decided — residents return specifically to look up their ballot before Election Day.

🏘️

On a local committee's site, the voter guide pre-filters automatically using that committee's search terms — visitors see their races immediately. See Local Committee Sites.

Legislative Tracker & Scorecards

Each entry is one official's action on a bill — and most entries arrive on their own.

An entry's plain-English fields: the official, their party and office, an Action Type (Accomplishment, Blocked, or Harmful — shown to the public as "Delivered / Blocked / Harmful"), a category (Education, Healthcare, Democracy…), a one-sentence description, and a link to the source. Vote entries can also carry how they voted, the tally, and the party split.

The weekly review workflow

An automated importer pulls new roll-call votes from LegiScan (Pennsylvania) and Congress.gov (federal) each week and drops them in as unpublished drafts flagged "Needs Review." Your job:

  1. Open the Legislative Tracker and look for entries marked 🆕 REVIEW.
  2. Confirm the framing and set the Action Type; check the source link.
  3. Turn "Needs Review" off, then Publish. Un-reviewed drafts never show on the public page.

Community tagging

Fill in the Municipalities Affected field with the boroughs or neighborhoods impacted — Fox Chapel, Mt. Lebanon, Squirrel Hill. Use the same spelling committees use in their voter-guide search terms. On a committee's site, the tracker then shows only that committee's entries by default (visitors can clear the filter).

📊

The official scorecards at /legislative-tracker/scorecards build themselves from these entries — you don't maintain them separately. Every tagged official gets a scorecard tallying delivered / blocked / harmful, each with its own shareable social card. Scorecards lead with the record, not the party: a bad record is highlighted, never buried.

Editing a Page

Who We Are, Get Involved, Run for Office, About, Vote, Contact, Become a Committee Member, and similar pages all live under Pages (Advanced) — and edit like a word processor.

  1. In the left menu, click Pages (Advanced) (at the bottom).
  2. Click the page you want to change — for example "Get Involved in ACDC."
  3. Edit any of: the Hero Headline (big title at the top), the Hero Subhead (the line under it), or the Page Content — paragraphs, headings, bold, bullet lists, links, and images.
  4. Click the green Publish button. Your change is live within a few minutes.
⚠️

Leave the "Slug (URL)" field alone on a page that's already live. That's the page's web address — changing it breaks existing links and menu items pointing at the page.

ℹ️

Editing an existing page is self-serve, as above. Creating a brand-new page at a new web address (and adding it to the menu) currently needs a quick hand from a developer.

People — Leadership & Elected Officials

The leadership grid on "Who We Are" and the Elected Officials page are both built from Committee & People → Committee Members. Each person is a single entry.

Elected officials are simply the entries whose "Ward / Municipality" field is set to elected-official.

To fix out-of-date info:

  1. Go to Committee & People → Committee Members.
  2. Use the search box at the top and type the person's name.
  3. Update the Title / Role, photo, or other details. If someone has left office, either change the entry to the new office-holder, or turn their "Active Member" toggle off to remove them from the site.
  4. Click Publish.
💡

To add a brand-new elected official, click Create new, fill in their name and title, and set "Ward / Municipality" to exactly elected-official — that's what puts them on the Elected Officials page. Then Publish.

🔐

A member's phone number is only shown publicly if their "Show Phone Publicly" toggle is on (off by default). It's always visible in the members-only roster.

Members-Only Content — Documents, Roster & Access

Everything in the members area at /members is managed from Committee & People.

  • Documents — go to 🔒 Internal Documents. Create an entry, upload the file (PDF, Word, etc.), give it a title, pick a category (Bylaws, Minutes, Forms, Training, or Other), and Publish. It appears in the members library, grouped by category. Turn a document's Active toggle off to hide it without deleting it.
  • The roster is your Committee Members list — the same records that power the public directory. Members see everyone's phone and email in the private roster, plus a CSV download.
  • Who can log in — a person can sign in (via one-time email link) only if their email is on an active Committee Members record. Site admins can be added separately. Set a departing member to inactive and their access ends.

Outreach — Vote-Reminder Sign-ups

When residents sign up through Remind Me to Vote, their details collect under Outreach → Vote Reminder Signups.

Each sign-up records the email, first name, ZIP, and how they plan to vote (by mail, in person, or undecided). You don't create these by hand — they come in from the public form. Open the list to review it or export it for your GOTV email or phone effort.

🚧

In progress — sending reminders isn't automated yet. Today the feature reliably captures sign-ups; how the reminder emails go out is pending a decision on integration. The options: (1) send from the site itself — pre-written reminder emails for the registration deadline, mail-ballot deadline, and Election Day are already built, and need only sender-domain verification and a schedule; (2) export the list into an email system ACDC already uses; or (3) integrate directly with that system. Until one is chosen, treat this as an email-capture list.
