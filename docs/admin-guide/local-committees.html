Local Committee Sites

Every local committee can have its own fully branded website — logo, colors, events, news, and voter guide — connected to the county site and managed from the same dashboard. This page covers how the sites work, how committees manage their own content, and how to launch a new one.

What a Committee Site Looks Like

A complete website under the committee's own branding — with a breadcrumb linking back to the county site, the committee's logo and colors in the navigation, and content scoped to that committee (plus any county content shared with them).

How Content Flows Between Sites

The county and committee sites are connected. County content can be shared down to committee sites. Committee content stays on their site. Nothing leaks where it shouldn't.

🏛 County Site

  • Election Day alert → all sites
  • Endorsement news → all sites
  • Canvassing training → all sites
  • County-only event → county only
→

🏘 Fox Chapel Site

  • FC monthly meeting
  • FC news & updates
  • FC voter guide (pre-filtered)
  • + Shared county content
→

🏘 Mt. Lebanon Site

  • MTL monthly meeting
  • MTL news & updates
  • MTL voter guide (pre-filtered)
  • + Shared county content
💡

A county admin creates one canvassing training event, flips "Share with all committees" on, and it instantly appears on every active committee site. Committee volunteers don't need to do anything.

Managing Your Committee's Content

Committees don't have to depend on the county for every update. Each committee can be given its own scoped login to the same dashboard.

Two Ways to Run a Committee Site

ModelHow it worksBest for
Self-managed (expected for most) The committee gets its own scoped login and posts its own events, news, and alerts. The county sets it up once. Committees with a volunteer who can spend a few minutes a month
County-managed The committee emails updates and a county admin posts them. Small committees, or a starting point before taking over
💰

Each self-managing committee needs one paid editor seat — see What It Costs.

What Each Menu Item Does

Menu itemWhat you manage there
Municipality Settings Your whole site identity: logo, accent color, homepage hero headline / subtext / image, contact info (email, phone, address — shown on your Contact page, falling back to the county's where blank), Facebook & Instagram, and footer tagline. Your menu inherits the county navigation automatically; use Hide Default Sections to drop items that don't apply, Additional Sections to append your own, or (advanced) Full Navigation Override to replace the menu entirely.
Action Alerts Banner alerts that appear only on your site. County-wide alerts show up automatically — you don't manage those.
Events Your committee's events. They appear on your site's calendar alongside county events shared with you.
News & Updates Your committee's news feed.
Pages Your committee's own versions of content pages — e.g. your own About or Who We Are. Until you create one, your site shows the county's page for that address; once you publish your own, it takes over.
Shared from County (read-only) A view of the county events and news currently shared to your site — so you always know what your visitors are seeing. You can't edit these; ask the county if something looks wrong.
⚠️

The same Edit → Publish rule applies as everywhere else: your changes save as private drafts and only go live when you click the green Publish button. If a change isn't showing up, you probably have an unpublished draft. Full details in Managing Content.

💡

Committee editors don't manage the voter guide or legislative tracker — those are county-maintained and appear on your site automatically, pre-filtered for your community via your voter-guide search terms.

Adding a New Committee — About 10 Minutes

A county admin fills out one form in the dashboard and clicks Publish. The site is live immediately — no technical help needed.

  1. In the dashboard, go to Municipalities and click Create new
  2. Enter the committee name, then click Generate to create the Slug (internal ID) — e.g. foxchapel. (Leave the slug alone after setup — it identifies all their content.)
  3. Toggle Active on
  4. Set a Subdomain Prefix (e.g. foxchapel → foxchapel.alleghenydems.com)
  5. Upload their logo and set their accent color (6-digit hex)
  6. Write a short hero headline and subtext for their homepage
  7. Under Technical, add their voter-guide search terms (borough name and/or ZIPs) so the voter guide pre-filters for their residents
  8. Click Publish — the site is live instantly
🚀

You can demo a site to a committee before they've committed to anything — create it, publish it, share the /municipalities/… link. No domain setup, no IT required.

If the committee will self-manage, the last step is asking the developer/admin to create their scoped editor login.
